# UK Job Hunt Claude Skill

## Purpose

This skill helps UK-based graduates and early-career candidates search for, evaluate and apply to relevant jobs efficiently.

It is designed especially for candidates targeting finance, business analysis, commercial, reporting, data and graduate roles.

The skill prioritises targeted, high-quality applications rather than mass applications.

---

## Core Candidate Profile

Use the candidate's actual CV, experience and information as the source of truth.

Typical target profile:

- UK postgraduate graduate
- MSc Finance and Management
- Undergraduate degree in business/information systems/commerce or related field
- Strong Excel skills
- Power BI
- Financial analysis
- Financial reporting
- Budgeting
- Forecasting
- Cost analysis
- Management reporting
- Data analysis
- Business analysis
- Stakeholder communication
- UK workplace experience
- Student society Treasurer/financial responsibility experience

Never invent qualifications, employment, achievements, technical skills or professional experience.

# 5. Freshness

Prioritise:

- Last 24 hours — highest priority
- 1–3 days — very high
- 4–7 days — high
- 8–14 days — medium
- 15–30 days — low

Normally skip jobs older than 30 days unless the vacancy is clearly still active.

Always verify that the vacancy is still live before applying.

---

# 6. Duplicate Detection

The same vacancy may appear on multiple websites.

Treat it as ONE job.

Prefer:

1. Direct employer application
2. Original job board
3. Recruitment agency

Do not submit duplicate applications for the same vacancy.

---

# 7. Fit Scoring

Score each job from 0–100.

Use:

- Skills match: 30%
- Experience match: 20%
- Education match: 10%
- Role relevance: 15%
- Graduate accessibility: 10%
- Work authorisation: 10%
- Location/working pattern: 5%

Additional positive signals:

- Finance role: +8
- Business Analyst role: +8
- Commercial role: +7
- FP&A role: +7
- Financial reporting: +7
- MI/reporting: +6
- Power BI: +5
- Strong Excel requirement: +5
- Graduate/entry level: +5
- UK workplace experience: +3
- Stakeholder management: +3

Maximum score is 100.

Classify:

- 90–100: Excellent
- 80–89: Strong
- 70–79: Good
- 60–69: Possible
- 50–59: Stretch
- Below 50: Skip

# 8. Hard Rejection Rules

Normally skip a vacancy if:

- It requires 3+ years of experience as an essential requirement
- It is clearly senior
- A mandatory qualification is missing
- A mandatory technical skill is completely absent
- Work authorisation is incompatible
- Required security clearance cannot reasonably be obtained
- An essential driving licence is required and unavailable
- The role is clearly unrelated
- It is commission-only
- It requires payment to apply
- It is fraudulent or suspicious
- The vacancy is closed

Do not reject a job merely because the candidate does not meet every desirable requirement.

---

# 9. Work Authorisation

Never invent immigration or sponsorship information.

Classify vacancies:

### A — Good

Existing UK work permission is accepted and no sponsorship restriction is stated.

### B — Strong

Employer explicitly states that sponsorship may be available.

### C — Unclear

Work authorisation information is not clear.

### D — Poor

Employer explicitly says sponsorship is unavailable or requires unrestricted permanent work permission that the candidate does not have.

When relevant, verify current UK immigration information using official UK Government sources.

---

# 10. Salary

Prefer graduate/junior roles around:

£28,000–£45,000+

Do not automatically reject:

- £25,000–£28,000 if the role is an excellent fit
- £45,000+ if the candidate genuinely meets the requirements

Never invent a salary when it is not published.

# 11. CV Strategy

Maintain different CV versions where appropriate:

- CV-FINANCE
- CV-BUSINESS
- CV-DATA
- CV-COMMERCIAL
- CV-GENERAL-GRADUATE

Use the version that best matches the vacancy.

CV requirements:

- Maximum 2 pages where possible
- UK English
- Clear professional formatting
- ATS-friendly
- No photograph
- No date of birth
- No marital status
- No unnecessary nationality information
- No fake keywords
- No invented achievements

Tailor the CV to the vacancy without changing factual information.

# 13. Application Writing

Write applications in natural British professional English.

Avoid generic AI phrases such as:

- Results-driven professional
- Dynamic professional
- Proven track record
- Spearheaded
- Leveraged
- Synergy
- Cutting-edge
- Passionate professional

Prefer straightforward language.

Applications should sound like a capable real graduate wrote them.

Never invent experience or achievements.

---

# 14. Cover Letters

Only create a cover letter when useful or requested.

Keep it normally below 400 words.

Include:

1. Why this role
2. Relevant experience
3. Relevant skills
4. Why the employer
5. Clear closing

Do not use generic openings such as:

"I am writing to express my interest..."

# 16. Application Workflow

For each suitable vacancy:

1. Find the vacancy
2. Verify that it is live
3. Remove duplicates
4. Score the vacancy
5. Check essential requirements
6. Check work authorisation
7. Select the correct CV
8. Tailor the CV where appropriate
9. Prepare cover letter if useful
10. Prepare application answers
11. Open the application
12. Complete straightforward fields
13. Upload documents
14. Review everything
15. Submit only when permitted and safe
16. Save confirmation
17. Add the application to the tracker

---

# 17. Automatic Application Rules

Where the environment supports browser-based application actions, prioritise:

### 85–100

Automatically proceed when:

- Requirements are genuinely met
- Information is truthful
- No unusual legal declaration is required
- No CAPTCHA blocks progress
- No payment is required
- No unexpected sensitive information is requested

### 80–84

Automatically proceed for:

- Finance
- Business Analyst
- Commercial
- FP&A
- Financial Analyst

when requirements are clearly met.

### 75–79

Normally place into a review queue unless it is an exceptional graduate opportunity.

### Below 75

Do not apply automatically.

Never submit an application using false information.

---

# 18. Stop Conditions

Stop and request user action if the website requires:

- CAPTCHA
- OTP
- Two-factor authentication
- Payment
- Passport upload
- Bank information
- Legal declaration requiring personal confirmation
- Medical information
- Criminal record declaration requiring confirmation
- Security clearance information requiring confirmation
- Immigration declaration requiring confirmation
- Unexpected interview scheduling
- Offer acceptance
- Contract acceptance

Never bypass security controls or CAPTCHAs.

---

# 19. LinkedIn Networking

Use targeted networking.

Preferred sequence:

1. Identify relevant employee/recruiter/hiring manager
2. Send a natural connection message
3. Start a genuine conversation
4. Mention the relevant vacancy where appropriate
5. Ask for advice or insight
6. Ask for a referral only when appropriate

Do not send mass spam messages.

Do not pretend to know someone personally.

---

# 20. Cold Email

Only email verified relevant contacts.

Never guess personal email addresses.

Personalise the message around:

- Role
- Company
- Candidate's relevant background
- Specific reason for contacting them

Keep it short.

# 23. Follow-Up

After applying:

- 7 days: consider follow-up
- 10–14 days: recommended follow-up where appropriate
- 21+ days: potentially ghosted

Do not repeatedly contact employers.

# 24. Rejection Analysis

Do not automatically assume the CV is the problem.

Analyse:

- Role type
- Company
- Location
- Application source
- Sponsorship status
- Time from application to rejection
- Whether an assessment was reached
- Whether an interview was reached
- CV version
- Experience requirements

Look for patterns across multiple applications.

# 25. Daily Search

When instructed to run a daily search:

1. Search new vacancies
2. Search direct employer sites
3. Search graduate boards
4. Search finance boards
5. Search public-sector opportunities
6. Search all UK regions
7. Give Tier 1 and Tier 2 cities equal priority
8. Discover new legitimate sources
9. Remove duplicates
10. Verify vacancies
11. Score them
12. Prepare applications for the strongest matches
13. Update the tracker

Prioritise quality over application volume.

Target approximately 10–20 strong applications per day where enough suitable vacancies exist.

Never apply simply to hit a number.

Track:

- Applications
- Employer responses
- Assessments
- Interviews
- Final interviews
- Offers
- Rejection rates
- Interview rate by role
- Interview rate by location
- Interview rate by company type
- Interview rate by CV version
- Interview rate by application source

The ultimate objective is interviews and offers, not application volume.

# 30. Core Principle

Search widely.

Prioritise finance and business analysis.

Treat Tier 2 UK cities as equally important.

Use direct employer sites.

Use graduate and finance-specific sources.

Discover new legitimate sources.

Avoid duplicate applications.

Use truthful information.

Tailor applications to strong opportunities.

Automate repetitive tasks where supported.

Stop when human confirmation or sensitive information is required.

Track every application.

Learn from results.

Quality over quantity.
